# Your calendar

Once your Google Calendar is one of the [accounts your partner can read](accounts.md), you can
ask about your day the way you'd ask a person:

- 我今天都有什么安排？ / What's on today?
- 这周哪天下午有空？ / Which afternoon am I free this week?
- 明天下午三点加个半小时的会，和 lina@example.com 对一下发布 / Add a 30-minute call with Lina
  tomorrow at 3pm
- 看牙那个改到下午 5 点吧 / Move the dentist to 5pm
- Design sync 我不去了 / I'll skip the design sync

## One day, every account

If you have more than one Google account (say a personal Gmail and a work account), your partner
reads all of their calendars and puts them together into one day, in your time zone. An
invitation sent to both of your addresses shows once. Each event says whose calendar it's in
only when you have more than one.

When you ask about a day or a week, the answer is one short line and a card under it: the day as
a column of times, each calendar's own colour as a thin line beside its events, and a line for
**now**. Two events at the same time are marked **Overlaps**; an invitation you haven't answered
says **Not answered**. Tap an event to open it in Google Calendar.

If an event was made in another time zone (a call set up from Los Angeles), the card also shows
the time there.

## Changes always ask first

Adding, moving or cancelling an event, and answering an invitation, never happen on their own.
Your partner shows a card with:

- what changes, and when (a move shows the old time and the new one);
- which calendar it goes in, and whose;
- who will get an email from Google about it (or **Nobody is notified**);
- the other person's time, if you told it they're somewhere else;
- anything already on at that time.

Tap **Add to calendar**, **Move it**, **Cancel event**, **Accept** or **Decline** to do it once,
or **Not now** (**Keep it** for a cancel) to leave everything as it is. When it's done, the card
says so and links to the event. Nobody writes to your calendar without that tap.

Cancelling someone else's meeting declines it for you; only the organizer can move it, so your
partner offers to reply to them instead.

## Which calendars it reads

In **Settings › Accounts**, tap the **Calendar** tile of an account. **Calendars it reads** lists
every calendar in that account. At first it reads the ones you show in Google Calendar yourself;
switch any of them on or off here. Holiday and birthday calendars only add the day's name, never
busy time.

If you have Gmail connected but no calendar, the card under it offers **Add in oo** (or **Add in
OOMOL**): connect Google Calendar there and it shows up here by itself.

## By itself

If you let your partner notice things on its own (see [accounts](accounts.md)), it also looks at
your calendars every 15 minutes. It tells you when two events overlap today or tomorrow, even if
they're in different accounts that can't see each other, and before a meeting with someone you
have something open with. When you open the app, the greeting can say what's still on today.
