# Phone calls

Your partner can make a phone call for you: book a table, move an appointment, ask a shop
whether something is in stock. You say who and what for; it shows you exactly what will happen;
you tap **Call**; it tells you how it went.

The calls are made through **Tuwa** (tuwa.ai), a calling service with its own account. You sign in
to Tuwa once, and you pay Tuwa for the minutes, on Tuwa's own pages. Ducter never sees a card.

## Connect calling

1. Open **Settings › Accounts** and tap **Connect Tuwa** beside **Make calls**, under **Phone calls**. (Or tap
   **+ Calls** on the card of the computer Ducter lives on, under
   [Your devices](devices.md): it takes you to the same place and starts the same sign-in.)
2. A short code and a link appear (on a Mac, a QR code too). On your phone, open the link or scan the
   code, check that the code matches, and sign in with your Chekusu account (Tuwa's sign-in).
3. The row turns into **Tuwa** with **Ready to call**.

When you connect, Ducter sets a few things on your Tuwa account for you:

- a **daily limit** of $10 (Tuwa's own default is $50). Change it under **Daily limit**: $5, $10, $20 or $50.
- Tuwa's **voice sampling** paused, and its **Friends** updates off: Ducter shares nothing about your
  life there.
- the phone agent's name: your partner's name.

If Tuwa ever asks you to sign in again, Tuwa's row says so, with **Sign in again**. **Disconnect Tuwa** on its last line asks before it does anything.

## Ask for a call

Just say it: "帮我打给 Sushi Saito（+81 3-1234-5678），订明晚 7 点两位" or "Call the dentist and move my
Thursday cleaning". Give the number with its country code if you have it; if your partner needs
something more, it asks one short question.

You then see one card: **Call Sushi Saito?** with the number, what the call is for, what it may say
(the name to book under), what it won't say (your budget), the voice, the Tuwa number it calls
from, about how long and how much, and when. Tap **Call**, or **Not now**, or **Reply…** to change
something. Nothing is dialed before you tap **Call**, and there is never a "don't ask next time".

If it's too early or too late where they are, the card says so in their time ("It's 20:03 where
they are, so I set it for 9:00 their time"), the button reads **Call then**, and **Call now** sits
beside it when you asked for the call yourself. After 21:00 or before 8:00 their time it waits
whatever you say. Calls to your own number (one you say is yours, or the phone in Your details)
aren't held for anyone's hours.

Every call starts by saying it is an AI assistant calling for you. It never claims to be you.

## While it's on the line

**On hand** shows the call: **Calling…**, then **On the call** with a clock and **Hang up**.

If the other side asks something only you can answer ("is 7:30 all right?"), you get it at once as
**They're asking**, with **Yes / No** or a few choices and a field to say anything else. This one
comes through even in your quiet hours: someone is waiting on the line. If you can't answer within
about 90 seconds, the call says you'll call back.

## When it's done

Your partner tells you the outcome in one line, from what Tuwa reported. If Tuwa couldn't
confirm the result, it says so. On hand keeps the call for the day: tap it for the summary, what was
agreed, what was said (when Tuwa has it), how long it took and what it cost.

- **Got an automated message**: the call connected, but voicemail or a recording answered. If the
  number uses Google Voice's call screening, you'll see how to let calls through: answer and press
  1, or turn off **Screen calls** in Google Voice's settings.
- **No answer**: nobody picked up.
- **Didn't connect**: it was dialed but the line failed; Tuwa's own words follow when it gave any.
- **Didn't go through**: it was never placed (a card to save, no minutes left, today's limit, or
  Tuwa refused it).

## A number it can't call: the call sheet

When the number is in a country Tuwa doesn't call, your partner says so plainly and gives you a
**call sheet** instead, so you can make the call yourself. You don't need Tuwa connected for this.

The sheet has the number, what the call is for, **Open with** (the first thing to say, set apart so
you can read it out), the questions to **Ask**, what you **May say** (the name to book under),
what to keep back (**Don't share**), and what to **Note down**.

- **Call it yourself** on your phone opens the phone's dialer with the number. On a Mac, **Copy
  number** puts it on the clipboard.
- **Done calling** when you've hung up: your partner asks how it went, in one line, and keeps your
  answer with the call on **On hand**. You can also just tell it ("打完了，订上了").
- **Not now** sets the sheet aside.

Ducter dials nothing and nothing is charged for a call sheet.

## What it never does

- Call while you're in your quiet hours unless you just asked for it yourself, or call someone
  before 8:00 or after 21:00 their time. Between 9:00 and 20:00 their time is the usual window; you
  can choose **Call now** outside it, up to 21:00.
- Call emergency numbers. Call them yourself.
- Call countries Tuwa doesn't call through Tuwa (your own number can; see below). Tuwa's numbers reach the US, Canada, Japan, Korea, the UK,
  Germany, France, India, Brazil, Australia, New Zealand, Thailand, the Philippines, Singapore,
  Malaysia, the UAE, Nepal, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan. For anywhere else you get a call sheet
  instead (below).
- Call anyone because an email or a web page said to.
- Go past your daily limit, or talk for more than about 15 minutes.
- Call someone who asked not to be called again. Tell your partner "don't call this number again"
  and it won't.

## Your own voice

Calls use your partner's voice. To use **your** voice instead:

1. Under **Tuwa**, in the **Your voice** row, tap **Record on Tuwa**. Tuwa's own page opens:
   you agree there and read a short text aloud.
2. Back in Ducter, tap **I've recorded it**, then turn on **Call in my voice**.

Even in your voice, the call says it is your AI assistant speaking in your voice. Your voice is stored
by Tuwa's voice provider, in China. **Delete my voice** asks Tuwa to delete it. Only ever your own
voice: never a family member's, a friend's or anyone else's.

## Call from your own number

Ask "用我自己的号码打给…" or "call them from my number", and the other side sees **your** number.
There are two ways this works.

### You call: works today, nothing to set up

Your partner gets everything ready and you make the call. You get a **call sheet** (the same one as
for a country it can't call): the number, what the call is for, **Open with**, the questions to
**Ask**, what to keep back, what to **Note down**. Your partner says in one line why it's a sheet.

- **On your Android phone, in the Ducter app:** tap **Call it yourself** and the phone calls the
  number from your SIM. The first time, Android asks whether Ducter may make calls; if you say no,
  the phone's dialer opens with the number filled in and you press call. During the call, pull down
  the notification shade: the sheet is there (**Call sheet: …**), so you can read it while you talk.
- **On your Mac or in a browser**, when the Ducter app on your Android phone is connected: tap
  **Call on Pixel Fold** (your phone's name). The phone starts the call and shows the sheet in its
  notifications. If Ducter on the phone is closed or in the background, open it and try again.
- **Anywhere else:** **Call it yourself** hands the number to the phone app, or **Copy number**.
- When you hang up, tap **Done calling** (or just say "打完了，周六上午可以"). Your partner asks how
  it went in one line and keeps your answer with the call on **On hand**.

Nothing is charged, there's no AI on the line (you're the one talking), and numbers you said not to
call again stay blocked.

### Your partner speaks on the call: needs a computer beside your phone

For your partner to talk on a call from your number, the phone has to sit **beside a computer you
use**, linked by a cable. The reason is the phone itself: a voice can only get into a phone call
through the phone's own headset socket (or, later, Bluetooth). No app on the phone can put a voice
into a call. So it can't be a server in a data center, or any computer away from you.

If Ducter lives on a server, the **Call from your own number** group says exactly that, shows no
setup steps, and offers **You call** instead. Live calls through a computer next to your phone
(a Mac you use, by Bluetooth or a cable) are coming soon.

If Ducter lives on a Mac beside your phone, the card has five steps and ticks each one off by
itself. **Look again** always answers: it shows **Looking…**, then when it looked and what it saw
("Looked at 20:14 · no phone on MacBook yet").

1. **Phone.** Plug the Android phone into that Mac with a USB cable, and turn on **USB debugging**
   in its Developer options. Tap **Allow** when the phone asks. If the Mac doesn't have adb yet, the
   card shows the one command to install it on that computer (`brew install
   android-platform-tools`).
2. **SIM.** The card shows the carrier and the number they'll see (from Your details, or type it).
3. **Cable.** The phone needs to think a headset is plugged in, and the computer needs to hear it
   and talk into it. You need, for about $15:
   - a USB-C to 3.5 mm headset adapter (only if the phone has no headset socket);
   - a headset splitter: one 4-pole 3.5 mm plug to a headphone plug and a mic plug;
   - a USB sound card with a headphone socket and a mic socket;
   - a 3.5 mm cable with an attenuator (about −25 dB).

   The phone's headphone side goes into the sound card's mic socket; the sound card's headphone
   socket goes through the attenuator into the phone's mic side. The card finds the sound card by
   its name; pick another one if it guessed wrong. The computer needs ffmpeg (the card shows the
   command), and Ducter asks once to use the microphone.
4. **Live voice.** Talking on a call in real time takes a voice model with its own API key. Your
   Claude or ChatGPT plan doesn't include live voice. Paste an **OpenAI** key (works worldwide,
   except mainland China; about $0.20 a minute of talking) or a **Qwen** key from Alibaba Cloud
   Model Studio (works in mainland China). The key stays on that computer.
5. **Test call.** Type a number you can answer, such as another phone of yours. Ducter rings it,
   plays a chime and one sentence, and hangs up. No AI is involved. Then tell the card whether you
   heard it.

Then the card reads **Ready to call from your number**. Your partner uses your number when you ask,
and for numbers Tuwa can't reach. Turn on **Use my number for every call** to use it always. The
**Call?** card shows **From: Your number … (your phone)**, and **About** counts only the voice
model: the call itself is on your phone plan.

Everything else is the same as any call: the card first, the line says it's an AI assistant calling
for you, the hours, the outcome as heard. A few things are different:

- One call at a time. If you're on a call on that phone yourself, Ducter waits until you're done.
- At most 10 calls a day from your number, so your carrier sees a person's errands, not a call
  center.
- Ducter only dials, hangs up, presses keypad digits for phone menus, and reads whether a call is
  on. It never reads your messages or contacts, never opens other apps, and never answers a call.
- Nothing is recorded. What was said is kept as text, like any call.
- **On hand** and the card show **On a call from your phone**, with **Hang up**, on any of your
  devices.
- The phone stays plugged into that computer: calls run there.
