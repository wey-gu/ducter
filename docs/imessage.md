# Talk to your partner in iMessage

You can text your partner from **Messages** on your iPhone, like any contact, and it answers
in the same thread. It's the same conversation you have in the Ducter app: what you say in
iMessage shows there too, and what it answers shows in both places.

This works through a **Mac**: only a Mac can send and receive iMessage, so the Ducter app on a
Mac does it. That Mac has to stay on and signed in, with the Ducter app open or in the menu bar.
It can be the Mac Ducter runs on, or a Mac connected to Ducter on a server.

## Two ways to set it up

- **Your partner's own Apple ID (recommended).** It's a contact of its own: a one-to-one thread,
  like texting a friend. It needs an Apple ID made for your partner, and a Mac where you don't use
  Messages yourself.
- **Message yourself.** Works right now with your own Apple ID: your partner talks only in the
  thread you keep with yourself, and everything it says starts with its name, so it never reads as
  you. But Messages shows every message in that thread twice, once sent and once received. That's
  how Messages shows a thread with yourself, and Ducter can't change it.

## Which Mac to use

Only one Apple ID can be signed in to Messages on a Mac (per macOS user account), and it doesn't
have to be the one in iCloud.

- **You have one Mac and don't use Messages on it yourself:** sign just **Messages** in to your
  partner's Apple ID. Your iCloud stays as it is, and your own iMessages keep coming to your phone.
- **You have one Mac and use Messages on it:** use **Message yourself**. A second macOS user account
  doesn't work for this: Messages can't send from a user that's switched to the background.
- **You have a spare Mac** (an old MacBook, a Mac mini), or Ducter runs on a server and you have a
  Mac: install Ducter on that Mac, connect it to your Ducter, sign its Messages in to your partner's
  Apple ID, and set up iMessage there. Keep it plugged in and awake; after it restarts, log in to it.

## Give your partner its own Apple ID

Settings › Chat apps › iMessage walks you through it, step by step:

1. **Make your partner an Apple ID** at account.apple.com, on any device. Use a new email address;
   the verification codes can go to your own phone number.
2. **On the Mac, sign Messages in to it:** Messages › Settings › iMessage, sign out, then sign in
   with your partner's Apple ID. Click **Check**: Ducter says which address Messages is signed in as.
   (The first time, macOS asks whether Ducter may use Messages; choose OK.) If that address is also
   the Mac's iCloud Apple ID, Ducter points it out, in case it's yours.
3. **Keep that Mac on**, with Ducter open.
4. **Connect** (below).

On your iPhone, add your partner's Apple ID as a contact, with its name and picture.

## Connect it

1. Open **Settings › Chat apps** in the Ducter app on that Mac. The third group is **iMessage**.
2. Choose whose Apple ID this Mac's Messages is signed in to: your partner's own Apple ID (it
   shows your partner's name), or **Message yourself**. Click **Connect**.
3. Give the app the two macOS permissions it asks for. Each row says what it allows; its
   **Allow** asks macOS or opens the right place in System Settings:
   - **Full Disk Access** lets Ducter read the iMessages sent to your partner. It reads only what
     arrives after you connect, never older messages.
   - **Automation › Messages** lets Ducter answer you in Messages. Tap **Allow** and say OK when
     macOS asks. It only ever writes to the one number or address you connect.
4. Settings shows a six-digit code. From your iPhone, text it to your partner's Apple ID (or, in
   Message yourself, to yourself). Your partner answers *We're connected*, and from then on you
   can just text it. If the code turns up in your thread with yourself, this Mac's Messages is your
   own Apple ID: nothing is connected, and Settings offers **Switch to Message yourself**.

The code works for 10 minutes, once. **New code** makes another.

## How it behaves there

- **What you send it** goes to your partner as your words, pictures included.
- **Its answers** to what you asked in iMessage come back to iMessage, results that come later too.
- **Something it notices by itself**, or a question it has for you, comes to iMessage only if you
  last talked to it there and you're not looking at the Ducter app on that Mac. It reaches you once:
  your phone isn't also notified about the same thing.
- **Quiet hours** hold here too: nothing it starts comes during them, and in the morning it sends
  only the newest question still waiting.
- **Questions with an easy yes or no**, ones that can be undone, end with *Reply "ok" … or "wait"…*.
  Reply **ok** (or 好) and it goes ahead; reply **wait** (or 先等等) and it holds. A Like or Dislike on
  that message works too. Only the newest such question is answered this way.
- **Anything bigger**, like something that reaches other people, your phone or your computers,
  says what it's about and asks you to open Ducter.
- **Money, sign-ins, passwords and keys are never in iMessage.** It only says something needs you
  in Ducter.
- **Someone else** who texts your partner's Apple ID gets no answer, and nothing they send is kept.
  Settings tells you, at most once a day, that someone tried. Group chats are never read.

If another Mac already carries iMessage for your Ducter, Settings on this Mac (and on the web)
says **On, at** that Mac, and there's nothing to connect here.

## If it stops working

Settings › Chat apps › iMessage says how it stands:

- **One permission to go**: one of the two permissions is off. Use its row to turn it on again.
- **This Mac's Messages isn't signed in to iMessage**: open Messages on the Mac and sign in.
- **This Mac's Messages looks like your own Apple ID**: your partner saw a message in its thread
  that it didn't send, so the Apple ID is probably yours. It stops talking until you choose
  **Switch to Message yourself**, or sign Messages in to your partner's own Apple ID.
- **Ducter is in a macOS user switched to the background**: Messages can't send from there. Keep
  that user in front, or use a Mac that is only for Ducter.
- **Can't reach iMessage right now**: it keeps trying by itself, and your phone gets its
  notifications as usual in the meantime.

**Disconnect** stops it at once. What you already said stays in your Ducter conversation.
