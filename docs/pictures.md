# Pictures

Your partner sees the pictures you send, the way a friend would in a messaging app. Ask about a
screenshot, send a photo of a whiteboard to work from, or ask it to draw something. When a task
makes something worth seeing (a screenshot that shows it works, a picture, a file), it shows up
in the conversation.

## Send a picture

- **On your phone:** tap the picture button left of where you type. Your phone's own menu opens:
  pick from your photos, take one, or choose a file. You can also paste a picture you copied.
- **On your Mac:** click the picture button, paste (⌘V), or drag pictures onto the conversation.
- **Up to 10 at a time.** Each one starts going up the moment you pick it, so Send is instant.
  Type words with them, or send them alone.
- **To a task:** open the task and send it pictures the same way, or drop them on the words when
  you start a **New task**. If an AI agent on the list can't see pictures, it says **Can't see
  images** there.

Before a picture leaves your phone, it is made smaller (2048 pixels on its long side) and
everything stored inside it is removed, including where it was taken.

If a picture doesn't go up (the connection dropped), your words and the picture stay where they
are, with **Didn't send · Retry**. Nothing you typed is lost.

## Ask for a drawing

Say "draw…" (画一张…) and your partner hands it to an AI agent that can make pictures. Today that
is Codex, signed in on one of your computers. The picture comes back in the conversation when it's
ready. If none of your computers has Codex signed in, your partner tells you so.

## Look at pictures

- **Tap a picture** to open it big. Pinch or double-tap to zoom, swipe left and right through
  every picture in the conversation (or in the task), and swipe down to close.
- **Save**, **Share** and **Copy** are along the bottom. A picture a task made says **From**
  and the task's name: tap it to open that task.
- **On your Mac:** Space opens and closes a picture, ← and → move between them, ⌘C copies it, and
  you can drag it to Finder.

## Read a file a task made

A file a task made (a report, a table, a script) shows as one line with its name and size. Tap it
and it opens right there in Ducter:

- **A report** in Markdown is laid out like a document, with its headings, tables and lists.
  **Show source** shows the text as it was written.
- **A table** (CSV) shows as a table.
- **Code and text** show with their colours and line numbers.
- **A PDF** shows its pages, one under the other; the top says how many there are.
- **Find** (the magnifier, or ⌘F / Ctrl+F) looks for words in a report, a table or code: every
  place they appear is marked, and the arrows (or Enter) go to the next one. On a Mac, a report
  or a table is searched in its text, and closing Find lays it out again.
- **Copy** copies the text, **Save a copy** keeps the whole file on your device (on a phone, your
  share sheet: save it to Files or send it to an app; on a Mac, your Downloads folder). **From**
  and the task's name opens the task.
- Close it with the ×, Esc, or by tapping outside it; on a phone, pull it down.

A very long file shows its first part and says so; Save a copy has all of it. Kinds Ducter can't
show yet (a Word or Excel file, a zip) say so, with **Save a copy**; on a Mac, **Open on this
Mac** opens it in its own app.

## Good to know

- Pictures stay on the computer Ducter lives on. A picture is only reachable through a link that
  your own devices get from Ducter, and that link stops working after about a day: one that ends
  up somewhere else (a browser's history, a message) soon opens nothing. Your devices get fresh
  links by themselves.
- Sending files and PDFs, sharing into Ducter from other apps, and video are coming soon.
