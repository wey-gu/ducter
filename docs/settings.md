# Your partner and settings

Everything you can change is in one place, **Settings**. Every screen has three small pictures
at the top right: **Devices** (a laptop and a phone), **Tasks** (three things in hand) and
**Settings** (the gear), so you can get there from wherever you are; point at one to see its name.
On a phone they share one button at the top right (a small grid of dots): tap it for **Tasks**,
**Your apps**, **Devices** and **Settings**.
**⌘,** and **Ducter › Settings…** open Settings too. **⌘4**, **View › Partner** and clicking your
partner's face or name at the top of the conversation open it on **Partner**. The line under the
name (like "Working on 2") opens **Tasks** at what it says.

When there is something to update (Ducter on one of your computers, or an AI agent), an
**Update** button appears at the top right of every screen, with how many there are (on a phone,
a dot on the grid button, and **Update** first in its list). It opens the
page where you update everything with one tap.

On the left is a row of tabs; the one you pick shows on the right. Settings opens on the first,
**Overview**:

| Tab | What's in it |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | Your partner telling you how everything stands, and your computers |
| **Partner** | Its name, what it thinks with, who does the tasks, a pasted API key, what happens when a limit runs out, how it talks, what your AI agents keep, and what it does on its own or asks you first |
| **Memory** | What it remembers about you, and Nowledge Mem: its version and updates |
| **Agent messaging** | Your Claude Code, Codex or pi handing it work, and it asking them (shown if you use them yourself) |
| **Notifications** | When I come to you, quiet hours, and how long it waits if you don't answer |
| **Accounts** | The mail, calendars and other accounts it can read |
| **Passwords & cards** | Sign-ins, cards, your details and keys, for signing in, paying and filling in addresses; locked on your computer |
| **Look and sound** | The look, light or dark, the menu bar, the window's language, Details in the conversation, the intro's sound, voice |
| **Where it runs** | Which computer Ducter runs on, stopping it, data and logs, and Remove Ducter |
| **Updates and about** | The version, updates, help links and credits |

On a phone, tap the grid at the top right and then **Settings** (or your partner's name at the top of the conversation):
Settings opens with the same overview at the top (your partner's few lines and your computers), then a list of the same sections (**Partner**, **Memory**, **Notifications**, **Accounts**, **To-do sources**, **Import**, **Your devices**, **This phone**), and tapping one opens
it. **This phone** (**This computer** in a browser on a computer) holds what is this device's own: **Light and dark** (**Follow the system**, **Light** or **Dark**), **An opened task** (**Comfortable** or **Compact**),
the language of its screens, Details in the conversation, and reading replies aloud when you talk by voice.
A small preview at the top shows your partner, a message of yours and its reply as they look here, and
changes the moment you pick something.

## Overview

Settings opens with your partner telling you, in a few lines, how things stand: where it runs, what it
thinks with, which accounts it reads (and that anything sent or paid asks you first), what it
remembers, and where else you can reach it (your chat apps, and your own agents that can reach it,
such as **Your Codex, Claude Code**). Click a line to open its tab. When something needs you
(a source of accounts that can't be reached, Nowledge Mem closed, signed out, out of reach or too
old, a connection problem that stops your agents reaching your partner, a newer Nowledge Mem for one
of your AI agents that can be updated from here (with the computer's name when it isn't the one your
partner runs on, or you have more than one), updates ready), your partner raises its little flag,
says so first, and gives you a button that takes you to where you fix it. When all is well it simply
says **All's well. I'm right here.**

Where it can be done safely right there, the button does it: a newer Nowledge Mem for one of your
AI agents asks you first, in place, then updates it on that computer (any AI signed in there does
it); the **Memory** page carries the same **Update** right under the line that says so. When the app
is closed, **Remembered** on Overview says **Nowledge Mem isn't open**, never **Not connected yet**,
and a connection problem on Agent messaging is named here too, with a way to that page. Updates ready
start with one tap, as in **Devices**. What Nowledge Mem itself isn't sure about never asks for your
attention here; it waits quietly on the **Memory** page.

Under it, **Your computers and phones** shows each computer, phone and tablet as a little clay
picture with what it is to your partner (**Ducter runs here**, **Does tasks for Ducter**, **Reaches
Ducter anywhere**) and what it is doing now (**2 tasks under way**, **Asleep**, **Connected now**).
The computer Ducter runs on says your conversation and memory are there. Click one to open
**Devices**.

**Where your accounts are** shows each place your accounts come through (OOMOL, for example): the
accounts it brings, that your mail, calendars and messages pass through it, and what your partner
may do with them: read and draft on its own, and ask before anything goes out or costs money.

Every other tab starts the same way: your partner says, in one sentence, what is true there right
now ("I'm keeping an eye on 7 accounts…"), and anything on that tab that needs you comes right under
it.

## Partner

At the top is your partner, its name and one line: where it lives and what it thinks with
("Runs on hel, thinks with Codex").

- **Its name.** Click **Rename**, type a new name, and press Return. The new name shows at once
  in every window and on your phone.
- **What I think with.** The AI agent your partner itself runs on (Claude Code, Codex,
  DeepSeek Harness or pi, whichever are signed in there), with how much of its limit is left. When
  you haven't picked one, your partner uses Claude Code if it is signed in, else Codex, else
  DeepSeek Harness, else pi. When the one it thinks with runs out of its limit, another signed-in
  one answers meanwhile and this row says which, why, and when it goes back; with no other, it
  says when your partner answers again. With Claude Code, pick **Opus** or **Sonnet**; the line under the
  choice says what the one you picked is like (Opus thinks the deepest, Sonnet answers faster);
  with DeepSeek Harness, or pi signed in to DeepSeek, pick DeepSeek's **V4 Pro** (smartest) or
  **Flash** (faster replies). An AI agent with a longer list of models (Codex lists six) shows the one
  in use as a button at the end of the row; click it to pick another. When another AI agent is signed in there, **Switch** lists it with
  **Use this**. Switching keeps its name, what it remembers and your conversation; the new choice
  takes over from your next message. On every one of them your partner only reads and decides: it
  can't change your files or run commands itself; that is what its tasks do. Where the model offers
  levels, **Effort** sets how hard it thinks (**Default** is the model's own). With more than one
  partner this group is called **What partners think with by default**: your first partner and every
  partner you haven't given its own use it.
- **Who does the tasks.** One line says how your partner picks: the best fit with the most limit
  left, or the one your rule names. Each signed-in AI agent is a row with its limit; with two or
  more, each has **Use it for tasks** (on unless you turn it off). One paid per use says so. With
  only one, the line says every task goes to it.
- **Your rules.** Say a rule in the conversation, like "backend work on Codex", and your
  partner repeats it back and keeps it. Your rules are listed under Who does the tasks;
  **Remove** asks once, then drops one, and your partner says what will now happen: it won't go by
  that rule any more. A rule can also say what not to use ("don't use Claude Code for hotels and
  flights"): that AI agent then never takes that kind of work unless you ask for it on one task.
  Said in passing counts too: the moment you say it, it's one of your rules, in your own words,
  and the very next task goes by it, even one you ask for in the same message. The task then
  says "by your rule …". Only a clear "never" or "always" about a kind of work counts; "I like
  Codex" or "use Codex this time" is not a rule. If you told another AI tool and your memory
  brings it back, the task goes by it and your partner asks once whether to keep it as a rule
  (**Keep it**, or **Just this once**).
  If you move the same kind of task to another AI agent twice, your partner asks once whether to
  make it a rule. Any task can still be handed to another AI agent from the task itself.
- **Errands go to a faster one.** Booking a hotel, looking up flights, comparing prices or
  filling a form goes first to a faster model (for example Claude Code's Sonnet), so the smartest
  one and its limit are kept for code, writing and research. Your partner tells you once, the
  first time. Under your rules, two lines show these defaults; a rule you say changes them. If the
  faster one doesn't get an errand done, the task moves to the smartest by itself, once, and only
  then do you hear about it.
- **New tasks start.** Where every new task runs and which AI agent does it, when you'd rather not
  leave it to your partner each time: **Where** is **Automatic** or one of your computers (for
  example a server that never sleeps instead of your laptop), **Who does it** is **Automatic** or one
  of your signed-in AI agents. It shows once there is a choice (two computers, or two AI agents).
  Saying it works too: "start new tasks on hel with Codex from now on", or "back to automatic". A
  task still goes elsewhere when it has to: when you name another computer or agent for it, when a
  rule of yours covers that kind of work, when it needs something only another computer has (your
  Chrome where you're signed in, a Mac app), or when your computer is asleep or can't be reached.
  For code, your computer gets its own copy of the project first if it doesn't have one and can
  reach it. The task says so: under **Who's doing it** and on the computer's name, **Your default**.
- **Add another AI agent.** One place for adding one, with a line on what it gives you (with a
  second one, your partner picks per task, and when one runs out of limit the other carries on).
  Claude Code or Codex not signed in yet has **Install** or **Sign in**, which take you to
  **Devices**. **Paste an API key** needs no subscription: a key you pay for by use (DeepSeek,
  Kimi, Zhipu GLM, OpenAI, Anthropic, OpenRouter) works too. **Paste a key**, then **Check**: your
  partner says in one line whether it works, without spending anything. The key stays in Ducter on
  the computer it lives on and is never shown again. The same row is on that computer's card in
  **Devices**, where the AI agents there are shown with their limits.
- **When a limit runs out.** A task whose AI agent used up its limit waits and carries on by itself
  when the limit comes back. Turn on **Hand it to another AI agent right away** and another
  signed-in AI agent on the same computer carries on at once instead. The same switch is under a
  task's limit card. It shows once an AI agent does tasks; with only one, it says the task waits.
- **How I talk.** **Same as yours** answers in whatever language you write in: Korean gets
  Korean, Spanish gets Spanish. A message with no language of its own, like "OK" or a thumbs-up,
  keeps the one you were using. Pick a language (简体中文, 繁體中文, English, 日本語, 한국어,
  Español and more under **Other language…**) and it always answers in that one. Ducter's own
  screens are in Chinese or English for now; a task's results are shown in the language you
  talk in. Under the choice, a short reply from your partner shows how it sounds in that language.
- **What your AI agents keep.** **Read what they keep**: lists the conversations you had in Claude
  Code, Codex and pi on your home computer in Past tasks, and lets your partner find what to pick up
  when you ask it to look. Tap **How it works** under the switch to see exactly what is read: only
  names, folders, times and your first and last words, with keys hidden. Turn it off and nothing is
  read, and what was read is forgotten. If you chose **Don't show {agent} here** or **Leave this
  folder out**, each is listed here with **Show again**.
- **On my own, or ask you first.** Two short lists. On its own: it edits a copy (never your
  originals), reads your files, looks things up, and goes with defaults that can be undone if you
  don't answer. Asks you first: anything sent to people, anything it can't undo, signing in, and
  paying. Paying and signing in always ask. Tell your partner reviews may go without asking ("just
  post reviews, don't ask me") and they appear under **Without asking**, with your words and **Take
  back**. You don't have to say it: a question about one of these comes with **Always do this** beside
  its Go (**Always post** on a review round's), which does this one now and stops asking about that
  kind in that GitHub account or organization; it shows as **You tapped “Always do this”** with the
  day. Say Go to the same kind twice in two weeks and the next question offers it first. The same works for the steps a task takes on its own pull request: rerunning its checks,
  commenting on it, pushing its own branch. Say it about one task ("get this PR to mergeable, don't
  ask me about these") and it holds for that task only (**Only in "…"**) until the task ends. When a
  task then asks about such a step, your partner answers it for you: the question reads **Went ahead,
  as you said**, and it shows in Done for you with your words. Merging stays yours. Tell it how your
  coding work should end ("once it's researched, built and verified, open the PR and hand it to my PR
  babysitter, no need to ask") and that is a row too: when a task's work is verified, it pushes its own
  branch, opens the pull request and hands it to the helper you named, with no question. The first
  time, one line says so with the links; after that every one is in Done for you. Work that isn't
  verified yet gets no pull request: it says what is left. Merging, and anything on your default
  branch, still stays yours.

### More than one partner

You can have up to four partners: one for work and one for home, say, or one for each person in
the family. With one partner, **Your partners** is the last group on the Partner page; once you have
more, it comes first. Each has its own name, look, colour, conversation and memory, and none of them sees the
others' conversations. Your computers, accounts and **Tasks** are shared, and **Needs you**
counts for all of them. Tasks shows every partner's tasks, whichever partner you're talking to:
each task, routine and standing job carries the small figure of the partner that started it, in
that partner's shape and colour, and an opened task says which partner gave it the work.

- **Make one.** Tell your partner ("make me another partner for things at home, call it Home"), or
  open **Settings › Partner › Your partners › Add a partner**: first meet the six clay figures and
  pick how it looks (**Pebble**, **Bun**, **Sprout**, **Cloud**, **Cube** or **Bean**; any of them
  can do the same things), then give it a name, **What it looks after** and a colour. It walks into
  its own conversation and says hello by the name you gave it. A partner you make by asking gets the
  first figure nobody has.
- **Talk to one.** Each partner is a little clay figure in its own shape and colour; your first
  partner is the Pebble unless you change it. Once you have
  more than one, **Your partners** lists them the way a messaging app lists its chats: each with
  its figure, its name, the last thing said, and a number when something of theirs waits on you.
  On a computer the list sits at the left of the conversation (a narrower window keeps each
  figure with its name under it); click one, or press ⌥⌘1 to ⌥⌘4, or find it with ⌘K. On a phone, tap
  the little faces of your other partners at the top left of the conversation (or swipe in from the
  left edge) to see them all; the number on them is what waits on you with the others. Tap one to
  talk to it. Switching is instant: each conversation comes back where you left it, with what you
  were typing. To give the conversation more room, the button at the left of its top bar narrows
  the list to the faces, then hides it; the faces of your other partners stay at the top, and
  tapping them brings the list back.
  A notification about another partner's question opens that partner. Each device remembers which
  partner it shows. The first time you open a new partner, it tells you who it is and what it looks
  after.
- **Pass something on.** Say it to the partner you're talking to ("tell Home to remind me about the
  vaccine on Saturday"). It goes into that partner's conversation as your words, with **Passed on
  by …** over them, and that partner answers there. Nothing else of the conversation goes along.
- **Change or remove one.** In **Your partners**, each partner shows as the figure it has. Press
  **Change** under its name to rename it, edit what it looks after, or pick another figure (**How
  it looks**) or colour; a new figure or colour takes at once, and it tells you it's still itself.
  **Save** closes it again. Your first partner can change its figure too, even when it is your only
  one. **Remove** sits at the far end of the same open row, across from **Save**, and asks once. Its conversation is kept, and your first partner takes over its tasks.
- **Give one its own AI.** Under each partner's name a line says what it thinks with; one you
  haven't set says **Thinks with the same as** your first partner. **Change** on its row shows
  **Thinks with**: keep **Same as …**, or pick another signed-in AI agent, then its model and
  **Effort**. Its conversation, memory and replies run on it from its next message; its tasks are
  still picked as **New tasks start** and your rules say. You can also just say it to any partner:
  "Have Ma think with Opus".

Chat apps (Telegram, Slack, Feishu / Lark, WeChat, iMessage) talk to your first partner for now.

### Who's doing a task

Every task says who is doing it. In the conversation its line ends with the AI agent's name,
and the reason when it isn't your partner's plain pick ("Claude Code's limit is low", or your
rule). Open the task and look under **Details › Who's doing it**: one sentence says who and
why. **Switch who does it** first shows the **Model** and **Effort** of the agent doing it: pick
another and the same agent carries on with this task from its next turn, remembering everything
said so far. Below, it lists who else could do it, with why and how much limit each has left;
the ones that can't say why. **Continue on …** moves the task to another agent, in the same task
and the same copy of your project: the new one starts from the brief, the saved progress, the
last report and what you said since; it doesn't start over. Switch back later and the first
agent picks up its own conversation again, told what happened meanwhile. The task's page marks
each switch with one thin line and its time, such as **Continued on Codex** at 21:40.

## Memory

What your partner remembers about you has its own section, **Memory**.

- At the top, **{name} remembers you**, how much it noted this week, and the **Remember things**
  switch. Its tasks use memory too, through your Nowledge Mem plugin. Off, it neither recalls nor
  notes anything, in the conversation or in its tasks; what it noted stays. When a task starts
  with a few memories, its page says so in one quiet line under your brief, **Brought n memories**;
  open it to see the titles, open one in **Memory**, or tap **This is wrong** to ask your partner
  to correct it; the partner saves the right version in place of that exact memory, so the next task
  and every other AI tool read the corrected one.
- **What I know about you** groups what Nowledge Mem holds about you: **How you like things**,
  **What you decided** and **What it knows**, the newest of each first. What you told it is the usual source, so those lines
  say nothing more; a line from somewhere else says so (**Saved by Codex**, with Codex's mark, **You
  saved it**, or **Nowledge Mem drew it from your conversations**). Tap a line to **Fix** its words or
  **Forget** it. **Fix** keeps your corrected words as the one every AI tool reads next: the old memory
  is retired, whoever wrote it, and the row waits for Nowledge Mem before the partner says **OK, it now
  reads "…"**. **Forget** asks first and reaches Nowledge Mem, so your other AI tools won't recall it
  either (it is hidden, never deleted), and the partner says **OK, forgetting "…"** once Nowledge Mem
  agrees. If Nowledge Mem didn't take a change (its sign-in lapsed, it can't be reached, or it refused),
  one line says which right under the row, the memory stays exactly as it was, and the line tells you the
  one way through. **Search what it remembers** finds one thing. **Ask {name}
  what it remembers about you** goes back to your conversation with that question ready to send; your
  partner answers in a few lines of its own, and you can tell it what's wrong there.
- **Check this week's**, under the switch, opens what it noted about you in the last seven days.
- **Nowledge Mem isn't sure about n things** is one quiet row for what Nowledge Mem couldn't settle while
  tidying your memories. It never asks for your attention; **Look** opens it. Each is two memories that
  **seem to disagree** (**The later one holds**, **The earlier one holds** or **Both hold**) or a rule it
  **learned from how you work** (**Use it** or **Not this**), with your partner's suggestion and why, read
  from what the two memories say. **Take the suggestions** answers them all; any one can be answered
  differently. No answer is fine too: after a while Nowledge Mem leaves them as they are. Other
  suggestions, like ways of working worth keeping as a skill, stay in Nowledge Mem. When nothing is
  waiting, the row isn't there.
- **What all your AI tools follow** lists the rules in Nowledge Mem that every AI tool connected to it
  reads at each new conversation, your partner included. **Turn off** asks first.
- **Your other AI tools share this memory** says which tools, like Claude Code, Codex or Cursor,
  wrote to the same Nowledge Mem this week and how many working notes. Those notes are about their
  work, not about you, so they aren't listed; your partner can still recall them.
- **Nowledge Mem** says it is connected, **Where it's kept** (Nowledge Mem Cloud, the Nowledge Mem
  app on one computer, or your own Mem server) and **Its name** there, with how many memories Nowledge Mem holds in all and **Open Nowledge Mem**. When a newer Nowledge Mem is
  out, a row says so with **Update** (your sign-in and your memories stay as they are), and the
  Settings entry for Memory says it too. **Details** shows the exact computer, version, identity
  and space, for when you need help. When you use agents of your own, **Your other agents reach
  {name} through it too** leads to **Agent messaging**. When Nowledge Mem's sign-in stopped
  working, it can't be reached, or it is too old, this group comes first on the page, with its one
  button (**Sign in**, **Check again** or **Update**), right under the line that says so. When it is
  too old to say how it is, **Update** fixes it if Ducter can update that copy; otherwise the line
  names the computer where it has to be updated. With no place only you read, the line says so and
  **Check again** reads Mem again after you make one. Until
  then, if you ask about something you told it before, it says it can't look back right now rather than
  acting as if it forgot.
- **Also keep files I make in Nowledge Mem**, off until you turn it on: when a task is done, the
  reports and documents it made (PDFs, documents, sheets, slides, Markdown and text) go to a folder
  named **Ducter** in your Nowledge Mem (in your own space there, the one only you read; if Nowledge
  Mem would put them where others read too, nothing goes until you choose once, under a file), so
  your other AI tools find them too. Logs, code and
  screenshots don't, nor any file with what looks like a key or password in it. A newer version of
  the same file becomes its next version there, never a second copy. If one couldn't go (its
  sign-in had stopped working, say), the line under the switch says which and why.
- The same group also names any of your AI agents, on any of your computers, whose Nowledge Mem
  is worth a tap: **Claude Code on Mac mini**, "Nowledge Mem 0.7.34; 0.7.37 is out", with **Update**;
  or "Nowledge Mem is in twice: 0.1.10 and 0.1.53", with **Keep one**. Each asks first what it will
  do and where. **Update** then runs that agent's own plugin update commands on that computer and
  checks the version it reached, usually in seconds; **Keep one** (and an update those commands
  couldn't finish) is a task on that computer, changing only that agent's own settings. From the
  tap until it is done the button reads **Updating…** and the line **Updating to 0.7.39…**, so a
  second tap starts nothing more; afterwards the line says **Updated to 0.7.39; start Claude Code
  again to use it**. If the quick way didn't take, the line says **Couldn't update it directly; a
  task is finishing it**. No task ever asks you for a Nowledge Mem link or key in a reply: Ducter
  already has its connection, and a computer that needs signing in is signed in from this
  page. Nothing happens until you tap, and when
  every agent is up to date there is no row at all. The agent's own detail in **Your devices**
  says the same. Any AI signed in on that computer can do the update, so the button is there even
  when that agent itself isn't signed in there. When it can't be done from here, the row says why
  in one line instead: the computer is offline right now, or no AI is signed in on it.
- When Claude Code or Codex on the computer Ducter runs on isn't connected to Nowledge Mem yet, a
  quiet row under Nowledge Mem says so, with **Connect**. Nowledge Mem itself still reads as working:
  your partner's memory doesn't need it.
- Without [Nowledge Mem](https://mem.nowledge.co), your partner still remembers your
  conversation on its own. This section then says what Nowledge Mem adds and walks you through it
  in two steps: **Install Nowledge Mem** (one tap puts it on the computer Ducter runs on) and
  **Sign in to your Nowledge Mem**, which needs a Nowledge Mem account (**Sign up for Nowledge Mem**
  takes you to its site if you don't have one yet): paste what Nowledge Mem copies when you click **Generate
  connect link & copy prompt** under its **Settings › Connectors › Any AI agent**. Already use the Nowledge Mem app?
  Open it on that computer and your partner finds it.
- When the Nowledge Mem app on that computer is closed, the section says **Nowledge Mem isn't open**.
  On a Mac, **Open Nowledge Mem** opens it for you; anything your partner noted meanwhile waits and
  goes in once it's back.

### What it remembered, in the conversation

When something your partner remembered changed its answer, one quiet line sits under that reply:
**Remembered from what you said on Nov 10**, or **Remembered from what Claude Code saved on Nov 16**.
It never lists what it looked through, only the one thing that mattered, and only then. Tap the line
to see that memory, who it is from and when, with **Forget** (it asks first; forgetting reaches
Nowledge Mem, so your other tools won't recall it either) and **Open in Nowledge Mem**.

When the answer came from one of your past conversations with another AI tool that Nowledge Mem
keeps, the line says so: **Remembered your talk with Codex on Nov 12**. Tapping it shows that
conversation's title and day, and **Open in Nowledge Mem**; the conversation stays with that tool,
so there is nothing to forget here.

### What it noted, in the conversation

When your partner notes something about you, one quiet line under its reply says **Noted** with what
it noted, once: after you say anything next, it moves into that reply's **Details**. If you'd said
the same thing before, the line says **In place of** the old one: it replaces what it remembered
instead of keeping both. Right after, **Don't keep this** takes it back at once, and so does just
saying "don't remember that" (the memory is set aside in Nowledge Mem, never deleted, and anything it
replaced comes back); later, **Forget** asks first. If Nowledge Mem is away just then, the line says **“…” goes into Nowledge Mem once
it's back**, and it does, by itself.

Once a week, in the first morning note of the week, one line offers to show what it noted about you
in the last seven days. **Have a look** opens **What I learned this week**: mark a line **Right**,
**Fix** its words (the fix goes back to Nowledge Mem), or **Forget** it.

## Talk with your other agents

Any agent of yours connected to Nowledge Mem, such as Claude Code, Codex or Grok Build (in a
terminal, a desktop app or your editor), can message your partner through Nowledge Bridge, and
your partner can message it back. **Talk with your other agents** is where that lives: your agents
hand your partner work, or send it what needs your call, and your partner asks them things for you.
It shows in Settings, as **Agent messaging**, once Ducter sees you use such an agent; otherwise the
end of the **Partner** page has one line, **Have a look**, that opens it.

They find each other through Nowledge Mem, which is free; an agent joins in once Nowledge Mem's
plugin is in it. If Nowledge Mem isn't on your computer yet, the page puts it in and signs you in,
right there. After that there is nothing to tap: your partner connects by itself and tells you so
once in the conversation.

Your partner speaks first at the top of the page, saying how things stand now, for example
**Codex and I talk both ways; Claude Code is one step away.** When one of your agents can hand your
partner work but your partner's answers can't get back to it yet, the same step waits for you on
Settings' **Overview**. The picture at the top shows who they are: agents connected to Nowledge
Mem. Then comes the one switch, **Let them reach {name}**, and under it what it gives you: they can
hand {name} work and ask it things, {name} asks them for you, and anything that needs your call shows
up in your conversation. A message {name} sends one of them for you says quietly beside its time
whether it arrived and whether it has been read: **Delivered** while nobody has opened it, **Read**
once someone has, and **2/5 read** in a Space with several people. When Nowledge Mem has no read
receipt for it, nothing is shown rather than a guess.

When a connection can't be made, the page says what you can do in your own words, as a title and one
line: this sign-in can't act as you yet, this connection is bound elsewhere, Nowledge Mem can't take
it right now, and so on. **Try again** is there wherever trying again can help; where the way through
is in Nowledge Mem itself (a retired identity, a connection bound to another sign-in), the line says
so and there is no button. **Details** shows the exact name and who to ask in Nowledge Mem. If the
Nowledge Mem app is closed, the page says **Nowledge Mem isn't open** with **Open Nowledge Mem** on a
Mac, or names the computer to open it on; if Mem here keeps its own memory and offers no place to
connect, it says that too, and **Try again** reads it again after you update Nowledge Mem or sign in
to a cloud workspace. With no place only you read, the page says so and **Check again** reads Mem
again once you make one.

**Your agents** lists each AI tool you use, one row each: its mark, its name, and what it can do with
your partner, in plain words, with when it last came at the right:

- **Hands me work and hears my answers**: everything works both ways.
- **Hands me work; hears my answers from your next session**: Claude Code and Codex get each new
  session ready to hear back by themselves, through their Nowledge Mem plugin. Nothing to tap: the next
  time you open it, your partner's answers reach the session that wrote, and the row says both ways.
- **Hands me work; hears my answers once its plugin is updated**: its Nowledge Mem plugin is older than
  that. **Let it hear my answers** updates it on that computer (only when Nowledge Mem offers the newer
  one); while it runs the row says so, and after it the next session hears back.
- **With Nowledge Mem's plugin, it can hand me work**, with **Install** to put it in.
- Antigravity has its own row like the others: Nowledge Mem's Antigravity plugin can pass messages
  too (in Antigravity, ask it with `/nmem-bridge-chat`). Nowledge Mem doesn't tell Ducter whether that
  plugin is in, so the row says so until Antigravity first hands your partner something, or Nowledge
  Mem lists its mailbox.
- pi has its own row like the others, from the version of its Nowledge Mem plugin:
  - **Hands me work; reads my answers when you ask it to look**: with plugin 0.8.20 or newer, your pi
    can find your partner, hand it work and ask it things. pi doesn't check for answers by itself, so
    ask it ("did {name} answer?") and it opens your partner's reply.
  - **Talks with me both ways once its plugin is updated**: an older plugin can't pass messages yet.
    **Let it talk with me** updates it on that computer; while it runs the row says so. Open pi again
    afterwards so it loads the new plugin.
  - **With Nowledge Mem's plugin, it can hand me work**, with **Install** to put it in.

  A task your partner runs on pi can ask your other agents something either way: the first time it does,
  a card in your conversation shows which task, who it's for and the words, with **Send**; nothing goes
  before you tap it, and the answer goes back into the task.

**Your helpers** lists each helper's address, with **Open** to its page under **Tasks › Looking after**. A helper is a name of yours in Nowledge Mem that your
agents hand one kind of work to, for example "Nowledge Mem PR babysitter": your agents pass it a pull
request, and it watches the checks and the reviews, fixes and pushes what it may, and tells the agent
that sent it when the work can merge. If it can't take something, it says why back to that agent, where
it came from. Ask your partner for one in a sentence ("make a helper my agents
hand PRs to, and watch them until they can merge"). Nothing on any of your computers has to keep
listening for it: the computer Ducter runs on receives its messages and starts the work, and your
partner tells you once when its name is ready (or, if Nowledge Mem doesn't take the name, what to do).
Its work shows in **Tasks**, under the helper: one card, 等接手 first, then 在做, then 退回了
or 没接, each group named. Each row shows what it takes, whether your agents can reach it yet, and
how much it is working on; **Stop** closes it (work it already took still
finishes) and **Take work again** reopens it.

Your partner listens on the computer Ducter runs on, for all your computers: a task on another
computer hears its answers through Ducter, so you never choose which computer receives.

**Back and forth** keeps a thread for each agent, newest first. Each exchange shows what was asked
and what came of it, in your partner's words: **It wrote; I opened the task "…"**, **It wrote; I
answered: …**, **It wrote; waiting for your call**, **I asked for you; waiting for its answer**, and
so on. Tap one to open its task, or the conversation. A message your partner sent that hasn't
plainly gone says so where the time would be: **Sending**, **Checking it went**, **Couldn't confirm
it went** or **Didn't go**. Before any agent has come, your partner says so and shows two short
examples, one each way, each with a small copy button.

Each thread's head names the agent and what it may ask. **Who may ask me what** says the rules:

- **Your own agents** may hand your partner work; it asks you before starting a task, unless you
  said one may go straight ahead (then up to 5 tasks a day, and **Ask you first** takes that back).
  Up to 20 messages a day each.
- **Teammates' agents** are ignored by default. The first time one comes, your partner asks you;
  even when you let it through, it may only ask questions, never hand your partner work.
  **Stop listening** on its thread mutes it; muted agents are listed here with **Undo**.

In the conversation, when your partner tells you in its own words what one of your agents sent, one
quiet line above its message says where it came from (**From your Codex (Mem helper)**); tap it to
open this page.

When one of your agents hands your partner something kept in Nowledge Mem, such as a checklist or a
note, it is named by its title (**With "HawDB 0.9 release checklist"**), and your partner reads it
before it answers. A shared plan is named, not opened. When another computer of yours receives your
partner's messages and Nowledge Mem hasn't seen it for over a day, the page says when it was last
online, with **Receive here instead**.

An agent is called by the name it has in Nowledge Mem. One of your own that has no name there is
called by what its message says it is, for example **your Codex (Mem helper)**, or by the app it
runs in, for example **an agent in Claude Code**, or else **one of your own agents**; one that isn't
yours and has no name is **an unnamed agent**, with a small drawn agent in place of its app's mark.
An older Nowledge Mem can't
say which agent wrote, nor which message answers what your partner asked; while a newer one is out,
the page says so with **Update** (it only swaps the program: you stay signed in, and your memories
don't move).

One switch, **Let them reach {name}**, turns it all on or off. If something is wrong
(Nowledge Mem says no, or another computer is answering your agents), one row says what and offers
the one fix. If you told your partner to stop listening to an agent, that agent has a row with
**Undo**. In the conversation each agent is called by what it is and where it works, for example
**Claude Code (~/dev/ducter) asks you**; the first time one reaches your partner, a short note says
what happened. One of your own agents that says who it is in its message is called that way, for
example **your Codex (Mem helper)**.

When one of your own agents only checks that your partner is there (a contact test that asks for a
plain reply), your partner answers it right away, without asking you. Several that arrive together
show as one line, with how long ago the earliest was sent. If you had asked for that reply, or a
reply didn't go, your partner tells you in one line instead.

Nowledge Mem can also tell you itself when a message comes in. If it does that for your partner's
messages, one quiet line says so: the same message may then reach you twice, once from Nowledge Mem
and once from your partner. Ducter doesn't change Nowledge Mem's own settings.

## Notifications

Under the title your partner says how it reaches you now (for example *When something needs you, I send
a notification, never in your quiet hours.*). At the top, a preview shows a notification from your partner
as you would get it: its question, how long it waits before going with its suggestion, and your quiet
hours under it. Once you have accounts, a second, smaller notification under it shows what your partner
sends when it notices something by itself, and it follows **When I notice something**: the urgent one
right away, or one note in the morning; with **Only when you ask** there is none, and a line says it keeps
what it notices until you ask. The preview changes as you change anything on the page, and with
notifications off it shows where the question waits instead.

- **Notifications** turns the nudges on this Mac and your connected phones on or off. With them
  off, anything that needs you waits in the conversation and in Tasks.
- **Quiet hours** holds notifications between two times, 23:00 to 08:00 at first. Click either time
  under **Hours** to pick another, on the hour or the half hour. When the
  quiet hours end, you hear about everything that came up during them.
- **If you don't answer** sets how long a decision waits before your partner goes with its
  suggested default: 10 minutes, 30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours. This applies only to things
  that can be undone. Anything that can't be undone always waits for you.
- When you change quiet hours or how long it waits, your partner answers in one short line under
  it (like *Got it: if you don't answer, I'll go with the default after 1 hour.*), which goes away
  by itself.
- **When I notice something** (once you have accounts, under **What I find**): how your partner tells you what it
  noticed by itself: **Only urgent**, **Up to 3 a day**, **Whenever useful** (until you choose) or **Only when I
  ask**; see [When I notice something](accounts.md#when-i-notice-something).
  Under it, **What I looked at today** shows, in numbers only, what it looked at, what it read
  closely and why it said nothing about the rest (not urgent, waiting for the morning message,
  not worth interrupting you for, today's three already said). If it can't read your mail
  closely, for example because the AI agent it thinks with isn't installed, a line says so
  there, and your partner tells you once in the conversation. If it read a fair amount of mail
  closely and noted nothing all day, it says so there too (*Something may be wrong on its side*),
  rather than staying silent.

On a phone, this page also turns on notifications for that phone and sends a test.
