# Tables, charts, lists and choices

Most of what your partner says is a sentence or two. When a reply reads better another way, it
shows a small table, chart, list or set of answers under its words. It only does this when you
asked for something that needs it, never just to decorate an answer.

- **A table** when you ask to compare things ("put the Mac mini and the MacBook Air side by
  side"). The things go across the top, what you compare them on goes down the side, and numbers
  line up on the right. On a phone, a wide table scrolls sideways inside its card.
- **A chart** when you ask about numbers over time or across a few things ("how long did CI take
  each day last week?"). It's a bar chart or a line, with the value over each bar or point, and a
  sentence above it that says what it shows. The numbers come from a task's report, your accounts
  or what you told it, never a guess.
- **A list to tick off** when you ask for one ("make me a list I can tick off"). Tap an item to
  tick it. Ticks stay on the device you ticked them on and aren't sent anywhere. If your partner
  asked to hear back, the list has **Send progress**: it sends what you've ticked and what's left
  as your reply.
- **Answers to tap** when your partner asks you something with a few short answers ("Which repo
  is it in?"). Tap one and it's sent as your reply, the same as typing it. Once you've answered,
  the answers stay under the question with yours marked, and can't be tapped again. You can always
  type something else instead.

For a choice about doing something (pushing, sending, paying), your partner still asks with its
usual buttons, never with answers to tap.

## Everywhere else

The same reply reads fine wherever it goes:

- **In your chat apps** (Telegram, Slack, WeChat, iMessage) and in **notifications**, a table is
  one line per row, a chart is its sentence and its numbers, and a list or the answers are lines
  starting with "-". Type your answer there as you would anyway.
- **Copy** copies those plain lines too, ready to paste anywhere.
- **An older version of the app** shows the plain lines instead of the table or chart. Update to
  see them drawn.

## Good to know

- Everything stays small: a table has at most 6 columns and 12 rows, a chart at most 14 points, a
  list at most 12 items, and there are at most 5 answers to tap.
- A screen reader reads a table as a table, and a chart as its sentence followed by each value.
- Tables and charts follow your light or dark appearance like the rest of the app.
