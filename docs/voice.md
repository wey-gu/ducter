# Talk by voice

You can say a message instead of typing it, and have your partner's messages read to you. Both use
the speech your Mac, phone or browser already has: nothing to install, no account, no key.

## Hold to talk

Beside **Send** there is a microphone.

1. **Hold it** and speak. Your partner leans in to listen, and the words appear as you say them.
2. **Let go**, and the words go into the message box. Fix anything you like, then send it.
3. **Slide up** before you let go to send it at once, or **slide left** to drop it.

A quick tap does nothing; it reminds you to hold. If nothing was heard, it says so and you can try
again.

**On the Mac** you can also hold a key while Ducter is the window in front: **Right Option** at
first. Change it in **Settings › Look and sound › Voice › Key to hold** (Fn, Right Option or None).
Let go of the key to put the words in the box; press **Esc** while holding to drop them. If Fn
also starts macOS's own dictation, set "Press Fn key to" to "Do Nothing" in System Settings,
Keyboard.

It listens in the language you're talking to your partner in. Hold to talk is in the main
conversation; a task's own message box doesn't have it yet (coming soon).

### The first time

The first time you hold the microphone, Ducter says in one line why it needs it and where the sound
goes. Choose **Use the microphone** and your device asks for permission. On a Mac and an iPhone
there are two permissions: the microphone and Speech Recognition.

If you said no before, Ducter tells you, and **Open Settings** takes you to the right place in the
iPhone, Android or Mac settings. In a browser, allow the microphone in the site settings beside the
address.

### Where the sound goes

Ducter never keeps the sound, and your Ducter never receives it: only the words you choose to send
leave your device. Who turns the sound into words depends on where you are, and Ducter says which
under the microphone the first time and in Settings:

| Where | Who turns your voice into words |
|---|---|
| Mac app, iPhone app | Your device, for languages Apple can recognize on the device. Otherwise Apple's speech recognition. |
| Android app | Your phone, when it can recognize your language on the phone (Android 12 and later). Otherwise the phone's speech service, usually Google's. |
| Chrome or Edge | The browser's speech recognition (Chrome's is Google's), or your computer when Chrome has your language installed for use on the device. |
| Safari | Apple's speech recognition. |
| Firefox | Not available: use Chrome or the Ducter app. |

A browser can only use the microphone when Ducter is opened over https (or on the same computer).

## Read aloud

To hear one of your partner's messages, long press it on a phone (or right click it on a computer)
and choose **Read aloud**. On a computer you can also point at the message and click the small
speaker beside it. **Stop reading** stops it, and so does starting to type.

It reads the message in the message's own language, with a voice from your device. Code is skipped
and links are read by their words, never their address. If your device has no voice for that
language, Ducter says so.

### Read replies aloud while you talk

Turn on **Read replies aloud when I talk by voice** (Settings › This phone on a phone, This computer in a
browser on a computer; Settings › Look and sound › Voice on the Mac). Then, after you send a message by voice,
your partner's reply to it is read to you on that device. It's off until you turn it on, and it
never reads anything you didn't just ask by voice.
